# Editorial policy

The email is a compact agenda for an economist focused on labour-market and economic research, not a reproduction of institutional calendars. The CSV is the broad archive: preserve all dated candidates retrieved from official sources, including secondary subjects and independently discovered events that are not selected for the email.

## Core priorities
- **Labour market (highest):** employment, unemployment, employment composition, hours, wages (nominal, real, contractual), labour costs, productivity, labour flows, job vacancies, matching/Beveridge curve, collective bargaining, labour policy, substantial institutional reports and meaningful international comparisons. Preserve novel analytical series even if infrequent.
- **Households and consumers:** key consumption, household income, purchasing power, consumer/business confidence and inflation releases. Prefer the principal Italian and euro-area figures, not every price index, sectoral breakdown, preliminary/final repetition or parallel update. Retain a second release only if it adds substantial information.
- **Major economic context:** GDP/national accounts, resident population/demographic dynamics, significant R&D/innovation measures, and other major structural findings.
- **Market services and trade:** prioritize high-value Italian retail, services/industry turnover, sectoral labour/productivity and selected tourism/accommodation indicators (e.g. nights spent). Do not fill the email with routine business, turnover, production or detailed tourism series.
- **Poverty:** absolute poverty in Italy and progress toward European absolute poverty measures are core. Relative poverty, deprivation, inequality and living conditions are secondary unless analytically important.

## Three classifications
- `high`: clearly important; core releases or substantial new evidence.
- `medium`: genuinely useful complement; include only where it earns space, particularly in the imminent week or where analytically novel (e.g. Beveridge curve).
- `calendar_only`: retain for reference, exclude from email and bulk ICS. Use this for dated discoveries not selected; if already in raw collection, no need to repeat them just to preserve them in CSV.

Do not classify a release high merely because it belongs to a relevant sector. For example, routine international tourism flows, preliminary structural business statistics, services production and services turnover at EU level are normally calendar-only; Italian combined industry/services turnover and accommodation nights may be useful. These examples calibrate judgement, not a whitelist or permanent blacklist.

## Selectivity
Aim for a noticeably shorter email than the initial 42-item pilot: roughly 15–25 useful items across the horizon is a soft editorial guide, not a quota or hard ceiling. The first week gets the most detail; weeks 2–3 prioritize substantive releases; days 22–30 only genuinely important advance notice. Avoid routine secondary updates, overlapping publications and multiple formats of the same release. Do not suppress a genuinely important event to meet a number. If several related series publish together, prefer the most useful representative entry. Keep titles and reasons short, dates sourced, and uncertainty separate from importance. Do not drop labour-market innovations, absolute poverty developments, or major institutional reports because they lack familiar keywords. Include significant unexpected announcements. The broad calendar is comprehensive only for material actually retrieved; clearly distinguish raw candidates and reviewed dates.
